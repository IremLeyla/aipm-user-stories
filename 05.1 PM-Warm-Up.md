
### Story 1: Show the source

**As a** support agent,
**I want** each answer to show which policy document it came from,
**so that** I can check it before I tell the customer.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. Every answer shows the document name and section it came from.
2. If no approved document has the answer, the assistant says "I couldn't find this in approved policy."

**Open question:** Who decides which documents are approved?

**Risk or assumption:** We assume the policy documents are up to date.

---

### Story 2: Review risky answers

**As a** team lead,
**I want** to check answers the assistant flags as risky,
**so that** wrong answers don't reach customers.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. Flagged answers are marked "Needs review" and can't be sent until a lead approves them.
2. Answers that need a disclaimer use the wording approved by legal.

**Open question:** How fast must a lead review a flagged answer?

**Risk or assumption:** If too many answers get flagged, leads won't keep up.

---

### Story 3: Prove it helps

**As a** pilot sponsor,
**I want** to see if agents answer faster and just as accurately with the assistant,
**so that** I can decide whether to keep using it.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. A report shows average answer time before and during the pilot.
2. The report shows the number of wrong or escalated answers before and during the pilot.

**Open question:** What counts as "success" for the pilot?

**Risk or assumption:** We assume we have data from before the pilot to compare against.

## Refined stories (after agent critique)

The originals above are kept for comparison. Decisions are recorded in [ai-collaboration-log.md](ai-collaboration-log.md).

### Story 1a: Cite the source

**As a** support agent,
**I want** each answer to cite and link the approved policy section it's based on,
**so that** I can verify it before replying to the customer.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. Given an answer, when it's displayed, then it shows the document title, the section and a link that opens that section.
2. Given an answer drawn from more than one document, then every source is listed.
3. Given a document that isn't on the approved list, then it is never cited.

**Assumptions to validate**

- The policy documents have sections or headings that can be cited.
- Someone owns the approved-document list and keeps it current.
- Agents will open the source when they're unsure.

---

### Story 2a: Review queue for flagged answers

**As a** team lead,
**I want** flagged answers to appear in a review queue,
**so that** I can approve or reject them before an agent uses them.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. Given a flagged answer, then the agent sees it marked "Needs review" and can't mark it as ready to send.
2. Given a flagged answer, then it appears in the lead's queue with the question, the answer and its sources.
3. Given a lead who approves or rejects an answer, then the agent sees that decision and any comment.

**Assumptions to validate**

- The assistant sits inside the workflow where answers get sent, so blocking is possible.
- Leads have capacity to review flagged answers.
- What counts as "risky" still needs to be defined.

---

### Story 3b: Pilot impact report

**As a** pilot sponsor,
**I want** a report comparing answer time and escalation rate before and during the pilot,
**so that** I can judge whether it meets the agreed success criteria.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. The report shows median handling time before and during the pilot for the same ticket types.
2. The report shows the escalation rate and the rate of answers judged wrong (using an agreed definition) for both periods.
3. The report states the date range and the number of tickets in each period.
4. Each metric shows whether it meets the agreed success threshold.

**Assumptions to validate**

- Comparable data from before the pilot exists and can be accessed.
- Four weeks produces enough tickets to compare.
- Someone is available to judge whether answers are wrong.
- Success criteria are agreed with the sponsor.
